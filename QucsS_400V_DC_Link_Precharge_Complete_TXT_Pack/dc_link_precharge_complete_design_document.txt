400 V DC-LINK PRECHARGE AND SAFE-DISCHARGE CIRCUIT
Complete Qucs-S / ngspice Design Document
=================================================

1. APPLICATION
--------------
The circuit represents the DC-link charging and discharge path of an inverter,
motor drive, charger, or industrial power converter.

The model verifies:
  - Controlled precharge of a 1,000 uF DC-link capacitor
  - Main-contactor transfer after sufficient precharge
  - Source, cable, switch, capacitor ESR, and capacitor ESL effects
  - Mechanical contactor delay and contact bounce
  - Safe discharge below 60 V
  - DC-link short-circuit behavior during precharge
  - Main-contactor weld behavior during shutdown

2. NOMINAL VALUES
-----------------
Source voltage:                 400 V
Source resistance:              250 mOhm
Cable/source inductance:        2 uH
Precharge resistor:             100 Ohm
Precharge-contact resistance:   20 mOhm
Main-contact resistance:        20 mOhm
DC-link capacitance:            1,000 uF
Capacitor ESR:                  25 mOhm
Capacitor ESL:                  20 nH
Discharge resistor:             4.7 kOhm
Discharge-contact resistance:   100 mOhm
Short-fault resistance:         50 mOhm

3. TOPOLOGY
-----------
400 V source:
  V_DC -> R_SOURCE -> L_SOURCE -> BUS_SRC

Main path:
  BUS_SRC -> S_MAIN -> V_MAIN_SNS -> VDC_LINK

Precharge path:
  BUS_SRC -> S_PRE -> V_PRE_SNS -> R_PRE -> VDC_LINK

Capacitor path:
  VDC_LINK -> L_ESL -> R_ESR -> C_DC -> GND

Discharge path:
  VDC_LINK -> S_DIS -> V_DIS_SNS -> R_DIS -> GND

Short-fault path:
  VDC_LINK -> S_SHORT -> V_SHORT_SNS -> R_SHORT -> GND

4. NOMINAL CALCULATIONS
-----------------------
Initial precharge current:

  I_PRE_0 = 400 / (100 + 0.25 + 0.02)
          = approximately 3.99 A

Initial precharge-resistor power:

  P_PRE_0 = I_PRE_0^2 x 100
          = approximately 1.59 kW

Effective precharge time constant:

  tau = (100 + 0.25 + 0.02) x 0.001
      = approximately 0.10027 s

Time to 90 percent:

  t90 = -tau x ln(0.1)
      = approximately 0.231 s

Time to 95 percent:

  t95 = -tau x ln(0.05)
      = approximately 0.300 s

Stored capacitor energy at 400 V:

  E_CAP = 0.5 x 0.001 x 400^2
        = 80 J

Ideal total series-resistor energy during charging is approximately 80 J.
The 100 Ohm precharge resistor receives nearly all of this energy because it
dominates the total charging resistance.

5. NOMINAL SEQUENCE
-------------------
Controller command:
  CMD_PRECHARGE rises at 10 ms.

Actual precharge contact:
  Begins closing at 30 ms after 20 ms pull-in delay.
  Bounces and becomes continuously closed at approximately 33 ms.

Controller command:
  CMD_MAIN rises at 340 ms.

Actual main contact:
  Begins closing at 360 ms after 20 ms pull-in delay.
  Bounces and becomes continuously closed at approximately 365 ms.

The expected DC-link voltage is above 380 V before first main-contact closure.

CMD_PRECHARGE falls at 390 ms.
The actual precharge contact opens at approximately 410 ms.

6. WHY 4.7 kOhm IS REQUIRED
----------------------------
For a capacitor discharging from 400 V to 60 V:

  t = R x C x ln(400 / 60)

With R = 10 kOhm and C = 1,000 uF:

  t = approximately 18.97 s

This fails the 10 s requirement.

With R = 4.7 kOhm:

  t = approximately 8.92 s

Initial discharge current:

  I_DIS_0 = 400 / 4700
          = approximately 85.1 mA

Initial discharge-resistor power:

  P_DIS_0 = 400^2 / 4700
          = approximately 34.0 W

The discharge resistor must tolerate approximately 34 W initially and nearly
80 J of stored capacitor energy during a normal discharge event.

7. CONTACTOR MODELLING
----------------------
The ngspice SW model represents the electrical contact only.

Mechanical effects are represented by separate signals:
  CMD_PRECHARGE and CONTACT_PRECHARGE
  CMD_MAIN and CONTACT_MAIN
  CMD_DISCHARGE and CONTACT_DISCHARGE

PWL voltage sources implement:
  - Pull-in delay
  - Release delay
  - Multiple contact-bounce transitions
  - Final stable state

The precharge switch must remain closed until CONTACT_MAIN is continuously
closed, not merely until CMD_MAIN rises.

8. NOMINAL ACCEPTANCE
---------------------
[ ] Peak precharge current <= 4.5 A
[ ] VDC_LINK >= 360 V at first main-contact closure
[ ] Preferred VDC_LINK >= 380 V at main closure
[ ] Precharge contact opens after stable main closure
[ ] Precharge current decays as the capacitor charges
[ ] Peak precharge power is reported
[ ] Precharge energy is close to 80 J
[ ] Main-contact bounce is visible
[ ] No repeated startup collapse occurs

9. SAFE-DISCHARGE ACCEPTANCE
----------------------------
[ ] Source contactors remain open during discharge
[ ] Discharge contact closes after shutdown
[ ] VDC_LINK falls below 60 V within 10 s
[ ] Peak discharge current is reported
[ ] Peak discharge power is reported
[ ] Discharge energy is reported

10. DC-LINK SHORT FAULT
-----------------------
The 50 mOhm fault branch is active before precharge.

Expected current:

  I_FAULT approximately 400 / (100 + 0.25 + 0.02 + 0.05)
          approximately 3.99 A

Expected precharge-resistor power:

  approximately 1.59 kW

The example controller aborts the command at 100 ms and the mechanical
precharge contact opens at 120 ms.

Required result:
  - Main contact remains open
  - Precharge is aborted
  - PRECHARGE_FAULT is asserted
  - Peak current, peak power, and integrated resistor energy are reported

The model cannot claim resistor survival without comparison against an actual
resistor pulse-energy specification.

11. MAIN-CONTACTOR WELD FAULT
-----------------------------
CONTACT_MAIN remains closed even though CMD_MAIN falls.

When discharge is enabled:
  - The source remains connected
  - VDC_LINK remains near 400 V
  - The 4.7 kOhm resistor dissipates approximately 34 W continuously
  - Safe discharge cannot succeed

Required result:
  - MAIN_CONTACTOR_WELD is asserted
  - Failure to reach 60 V is reported
  - Continuous resistor power and accumulated energy are measured
  - The event is reported as a fault, not a successful discharge

12. Qucs-S COMPONENT MAPPING
----------------------------
Use native Qucs-S components for:
  - Voltage sources
  - Resistors
  - Inductors
  - Capacitors
  - Controlled switches
  - Ground
  - Probes
  - Transient simulation blocks
  - Equations and display plots

Load the switch models from:

  .include "./dc_link_precharge_models.txt"

Controlled-switch terminal mapping:

  Power terminal 1
  Power terminal 2
  Control positive
  Control negative

The control negative terminal is connected to GND.

15. LIMITATIONS
---------------
The model does not validate:
  - Real contactor coil dynamics
  - Contact arc formation
  - Contact erosion
  - Fuse opening
  - Resistor temperature rise
  - Resistor pulse-energy survival
  - Capacitor tolerance and temperature dependence
  - Insulation and creepage requirements
  - Hardware high-voltage safety certification
